The Accounting Equation

Every transaction you record in the simulator must keep this balanced. QBO enforces it — but knowing why helps you catch errors instantly.

Assets
What you OWN
Cash · AR · Equipment
=
Liabilities
What you OWE
AP · Loans · Tax Payable
+
Equity
Owner's Share
Capital · Retained Earnings
Every journal entry changes at least two accounts. The equation must always stay balanced — this is why Debits always equal Credits.

↕️ Quick Recall · 02

Debit & Credit — The One Table to Remember

Debit = Left. Credit = Right. Whether it increases or decreases depends on the account type. Use DEAD CLIC to remember which direction increases each type.

Account Type Normal Balance Debit Does Credit Does Examples
Assets DR ⬆️ Increases ⬇️ Decreases Cash · AR · Equipment · Inventory
Liabilities CR ⬇️ Decreases ⬆️ Increases AP · Loans · Sales Tax Payable
Equity CR ⬇️ Decreases ⬆️ Increases Owner's Capital · Retained Earnings
Revenue / Income CR ⬇️ Decreases ⬆️ Increases Sales · Service Income · Interest
Expenses DR ⬆️ Increases ⬇️ Decreases Rent · Payroll · Ads · Utilities
💡
DEAD CLIC memory trick: DR increases Dividends, Expenses, Assets, Drawings. CR increases Capital, Liabilities, Income, Contributions.

What Happens When You Record This?

In the simulator, every action changes accounts and reports. This is the cheat sheet — keep it in mind as you click.

🧾 Record a Sale
DR Accounts Receivable CR Sales Revenue
P&L Revenue ⬆️ BS: AR ⬆️
💵 Receive Payment
DR Cash / Bank CR Accounts Receivable
BS: Cash ⬆️ BS: AR ⬇️ Cash Flow ⬆️
📦 Pay an Expense
DR Expense Account CR Cash / Bank
P&L Expenses ⬆️ Net Income ⬇️ BS: Cash ⬇️
🏦 Bank Reconciliation
Match QBO transactions to bank statement
Difference = $0.00 ✅ BS Cash is accurate
👷 Record Payroll
DR Payroll Expense (gross) CR Cash (net) + Tax Liabilities
P&L Payroll ⬆️ BS: Tax Liab ⬆️ BS: Cash ⬇️

🧠 Mindset · 06

The Right Mindset for the Simulator

The simulator is designed to be realistic — which means it can feel messy. Here's the difference between students who finish with confidence and students who give up.

❌ The Wrong Approach
  • Rushing to click without reading
  • Panicking when the balance is off
  • Treating errors as failures
  • Skipping the "why" to get to the answer
  • Comparing your pace to others
✅ The Smart Approach
  • Read each transaction before recording
  • A wrong balance = a clue, not a failure
  • Errors are the lesson — find and fix them
  • Understand each entry before moving on
  • Your own pace is the right pace
💛
From Ate Mic: The most successful students aren't the fastest ones. They're the ones who stop, ask "why is this balance off?", and figure it out. That habit is exactly what US clients are paying for.
